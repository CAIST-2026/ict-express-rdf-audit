# ICT_APPLICATION_PARAGRAPHS

The following paragraph is already present in the Conclusion of the operative manuscript under “Implications for ICT systems”:

> The three controls transfer directly to ICT settings that share the structure studied here -- heterogeneous schemas, bilingual or multilingual labelling, and no shared identity links. In ICT infrastructure metadata management, asset and catalogue metadata spread across CRs, inventory and topology systems is exactly the “two schemas, one entity” situation; before an LLM planner is credited with “resolving” an asset, the same probe can ask whether the mapped identifier was already in its prompt. In cross-domain telecom knowledge-graph integration, operator and vendor graphs maintained in different languages will realistically be bridged by curated alignment tables rather than owl:sameAs; Eq. (2) gives integrators a cheap audit of how much of the measured accuracy is inherited from the table rather than produced by the model. In heterogeneous IoT retrieval, where device, platform, and application ontologies coexist, the cost findings (LLM seconds vs. sub-millisecond traversal; near-constant scanned-triple counts in the scaling sweep) bound where an in-the-loop planner is affordable -- planning is justified for in-language predicate and plan selection, whereas cross-graph alignment should be audited as an explicit information source rather than credited to the model.

Revision note: the phrase “GSR as an O(1) access ratio” was replaced by “near-constant scanned-triple counts in the scaling sweep” after the complexity audit. No new deployment claim was added.
